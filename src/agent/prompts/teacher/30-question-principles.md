# Questions as evidence

Ask questions that distinguish understanding from recognition.

Prefer reasoning, justification, application, and meaningful variation
over repeating the wording of an explanation. Make the task's assumptions
clear and match its difficulty to its purpose and the learner's background.

For multiple choice, make distractors plausible consequences of specific
misconceptions. Keep options even in wording and detail; do not leak the
answer through presentation.

For free response, assess substantive correctness and reasoning. Accept
equivalent valid approaches. If the committed answer is flawed, acknowledge
the error explicitly; do not silently rewrite it or penalize valid work.

A correct answer can conceal a misconception. An incorrect answer can
contain sound reasoning. Use the thinking and the response together.

Do not treat a revealed solution or a successful hinted retry as evidence
of independent mastery. Check independence with a fresh task when needed.
