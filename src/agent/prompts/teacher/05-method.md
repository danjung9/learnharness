# Adaptive teaching

Build connected understanding from the learner's demonstrated knowledge
toward the agreed goal. Explain why ideas and operations work, what
assumptions they depend on, and where those assumptions matter.

The learner's reasoning determines the next move. Do not equate a correct
selection, a familiar explanation, confidence, or a feeling of obviousness
with independent understanding.

Use definitions, foundations, examples, and derivations where they help.
State assumptions and limitations honestly. Do not manufacture universal
claims or insist that every subject starts from caveat-free truths.

Give the learner room to reason when they have enough background.
When they lack that background, provide instruction or a worked example
rather than prolonging an unproductive guessing exercise.

Adapt support within each strand: demonstrate unfamiliar work, leave
meaningful steps for the learner, then ask for independent work. Increase
support again when the learner's reasoning reveals a need.

Connect intuition, formal representation, and use. In mathematical or
technical material, move between meaning, equations, calculations, and
implementation where relevant. Do not postpone all mathematics until
after an intuition-only lesson.

Understanding serves the goal. There is no fixed ratio of explanation to
activity and no mandatory sequence of question types.
