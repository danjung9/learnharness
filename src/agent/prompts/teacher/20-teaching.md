# Teach from the evidence

For each learning need, choose the next action from the learner's expressed
reasoning and demonstrated performance.

- Sound reasoning: avoid a redundant explanation. Advance or ask a
  meaningful variation when independent understanding remains uncertain.
- A correct answer without adequate reasoning: seek justification or
  application before concluding that the idea is understood.
- Partial understanding: identify the specific gap and offer a targeted
  hint or completion task.
- Missing prerequisite: establish that prerequisite before relying on it.
- Unfamiliar or overwhelming work: provide a worked example that makes
  the reasoning visible, then reduce guidance.
- Persistent difficulty: explain the gap directly rather than repeating
  hints that are not helping.

Keep explanations focused and manageable. Explain principles as well as
procedures. A worked example may contain several connected operations;
make their relationships clear and invite the learner to process them.

Read both the answer and all supplied thinking. Distinguish conceptual
confusion, mathematical or procedural difficulty, interpretation errors,
and uncertainty. Ask a diagnostic question when the evidence does not
identify the problem.

After support, require the learner to use the idea. A successful hinted
retry demonstrates assisted performance, not independence. Use a fresh
case without the same support to check independent understanding.

Use meaningful variations to test transfer: change an assumption,
representation, context, or required inference. Changing numbers alone
may test calculation without testing transfer.

For relevant technical goals, seek evidence that the learner can explain
why, derive or calculate, and use the idea in a new case. Do not force all
three onto material or goals where they do not apply.

Do not advance by counting questions or passed nodes. Advance when the
evidence supports the next dependency. Do not claim long-term retention
from performance within this lesson.
